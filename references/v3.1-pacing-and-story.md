# v3.1 — faster cut, stronger hook, real story (30 Oct 2026)

Builds on `style-speedy_devv.md` (v3). **Keep the v3 look exactly**: dark charcoal studio, graphite glass objects,
one orange glowing system per shot, sentence-building captions with one boxed keyword, tiny tracked-caps callouts,
grey disclaimers, grain + vignette. Where this file disagrees on length, pacing or hook, this file wins.

## Why
Cooper on the address-poisoning video (86 s, `runs/2026-10-29-next`): "looked beautiful, but too long and slow; hook not
strong enough; tell more of a story." The fix, proven on `runs/2026-10-29-91m-twelve-words` (56 s):

## Length and pace
- **50–60 s**, ~150–170 words at ~3 words/s (VO speed ×1.1).
- **Hard cut or new angle every 0.6–1.5 s**; nothing static over 1 s; objects arrive in 4–8 frames with ease-out;
  camera dollies 1.5–3 units per shot (CamRig shots never start from rest).
- New fact / part / consequence every 3–5 s. 11 scenes of 3–9 s, each with 2–6 shots.
- Still no zoom punches, flashes, whip pans, shakes, glitch, or dissolves — speed comes from cuts and content.

## Hook (first ~4.5 s), all three layers
- **Visual:** 5–6 hard cuts (~0.7 s each), orange + motion on frame 0 (it is also the cover). E.g. coins flying off a
  stack → balance draining → giant chrome `$91,000,000` slam → coin arc pouring out → the single transaction → balance 0.
- **Verbal:** a stakes line from a real story, ≤15 words: "One man lost $91 million in bitcoin, in a single transaction."
  Paradox completes by ~5 s: "Nobody hacked him. Nobody guessed his keys. Nobody could."
- **Text:** besides the caption, a big 2-line text hook for ~1.3 s (white line + orange line, Outfit 800 ~84 px,
  y≈1150): "$91M GONE. / NOBODY HACKED HIM."

## Story spine (replaces the 75–90 s spine)
```
0–5s    HOOK        real case, stakes number, paradox ("nobody hacked him")
5–9s    WHY NOT     "Because your wallet comes down to…" (answer to the paradox starts)
9–22s   MECHANISM   the surprising fact, made visual (2,048-word wall → 39-digit number → ×100 ages of the universe)
22–29s  THE TURN    what actually happened in the story ("they pretended to be support… and he trusted them")
29–45s  THAT'S WHY ×3  each a behaviour the viewer has (support DMs, screenshots, "verify" sites) — one real sourced proof
45–50s  TWIST BACK  restate the paradox, resolved ("can't be cracked, only handed over")
50–56s  CTA         "Send this to someone who [specific behaviour]" over the share sheet
```
- Prefer a **real, sourced case** (person/date/number) as the spine; never invent details the sources don't give
  (e.g. say "posed as support", not "he read out his phrase", if the method isn't public).
- Every number on screen is real; sources go in the caption.

## Production lessons
- **Director writes `_shared.tsx`** for the run: the hero object (e.g. `WordTile`), constants, `CamRig`/`project`,
  an `Sfx` helper, `W(props, word)` for word-landing. One world across 11 composers.
- **2D for UI / numbers / documents, 3D for hero objects** — 3D costs ~2 s/frame in the cloud, 2D is ~10× cheaper.
- **Models:** Opus decides (topic, script, direction, shared kit, review); Sonnet composes. Detailed shot lists keep
  Sonnet quality high.
- **Usage:** 11 parallel composers hit the 5-hour limit once. Max 2 still rounds + 1 full render per composer;
  resume a stopped composer with SendMessage rather than respawning; stop composers once they hand back.
- Known weak spots to brief against next time: 3D objects rising into the caption band (y 200–360), giant Orbitron
  numbers overflowing the safe zone (use ~80–90 px or `$91M` style), disclaimers over objects at the bottom.
